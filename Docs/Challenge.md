# Field Shift: Adapting Farms with NASA Data

Farmers around the world navigate **shifting temperatures**, **changing rainfall patterns**, **water shortages**, **extreme weather**, and **declining soil health**. These pressures make it difficult to choose crop rotations that protect fields, conserve water, and support long‑term resilience. Your challenge is to create a **decision‑support tool** that uses NASA Earth observations along with local soil information, crop characteristics, and farmer priorities to help farmers explore rotation strategies that could strengthen soil health and adapt their farms to changing conditions.

## What is Crop Rotation actually?

Crop rotation is ==the agricultural practice of planting different crop species sequentially on the same plot of land over successive growing seasons to maintain soil health and manage pests==

The value and efficiency of a crop rotation depends on several factors, including **crop types used in rotation** ([Tiemann et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2351989420304790#bib119)), **rotating series** and **applied frequency of certain crops** ([Bainard et al., 2017b](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2351989420304790#bib9)), **rotating length** ([Bennett et al., 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2351989420304790#bib15)), **agronomic history on farmland** and **soil characteristics** ([Li et al., 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2351989420304790#bib72)).

Certain crops are better in rotation than others, making it difficult to determine the best rotation sequence to maximize soil benefits.
