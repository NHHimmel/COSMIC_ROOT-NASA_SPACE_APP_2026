# Lesson 1: What is a farm ecosystem?

## Definition
A **farm ecosystem (agroecosystem)** is an ecosystem in which living organisms and non-living components interact, with humans actively managing many of those interactions for agricultural production.

## Interaction
The farm ecosystem is different from a forest or a meadow ecosystem because **humans control many of the interactions** among the things on the farm. 
Farmers need to know how the things on their farms interact, or the **ecology** of their farms, in order to make choices about how to raise their crops and animals in the farm environment.

## Components

» Crops – plants cultivated for the benefit of humankind; 
» Weeds – plants that are potential competitors to crops; 
» Pests - animal predators and parasites; 
» Pathogens – micro-organisms causing diseases; 
» domestic animals; 
» Beneficial micro-organisms - e.g. rhizobia and other nitrogen fixing bacteria, mycorrhizal fungi; 
» Beneficial arthropods - pollinators, natural enemies of pests; 
» Soil. 

> Note: 
> - Farm ecosystem retains most of the functional properties of the natural ecosystem
> - Dominant plants and animals species are artificial rather than natural selection.
> - Human inputs determine the population size - linked to the productivity of the ecosystem.

## Why it matters for crop rotation

Understanding farm ecosystem is crucial for crop rotation because in a farm ecosystem is maintained by human rather than complete natural process. So, a farm ecosystem's land size, soil quality, weather, water condition, plants, etc. interactions influence which crops can be grown successfully and which management choices may be appropriate in a particular season.

## Key Takeaway
**A farm is a managed ecosystem, not an isolated crop-production system.** Crop choice affects and is affected by soil, water, climate, organisms and previous management decisions.

## **Connection to AI/ML:** 
The farm ecosystem resembles a sequential decision-making system - **Markov Decision Process (MDP):** the current farm state influences available actions, actions affect future farm states, and environmental factors introduce uncertainty.
